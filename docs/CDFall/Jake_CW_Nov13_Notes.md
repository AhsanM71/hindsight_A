## Cognitive Walkthrough Notes

### Title Screen
- The waving animation is distracting and could be misleading.
- The Play button will most likely be pressed, so this screen works overall.

### Game Saves
- Clicking a character to get into the game makes sense.
- Kids might not know to press their character picture, since they can't read the name.
  - Their first instinct might be to press the green Home button instead.

### Level Menu
- The green button draws attention.
- The giant purple button also catches kids' interest.
- Three-year-olds might not realize the page should be read from top left to bottom right.

### Character Menu
- The character layout is pretty intuitive.
- Switching sections in the character menu is clear and makes sense.
- Parents would understand the coins, but kids might not understand what they do.
- Some kids might get stuck on the character selection screen because they don't know to press the Back button.

### Game

**Layout and buttons**
- The button colouring at the bottom is good.
- The progress bar at the top might distract kids, and they might want to click it as it changes.
- The green arrow and the green menu button could be confused with each other.

**Clicking on Sam**
- Kids might get confused and click the volume button instead of Sam.
- The floating yellow arrow worked well, but the progress bar cuts it off.
- Kids would probably click the green button instead of clicking Sam again.

**Eye exam sequence**
- The pupil enlargement explanation was well done and intuitive.
- It's unclear whether kids will understand that the eye drops cause some discomfort.
  - The screen dims when the drops go in. That's a good cue that *something* is happening, though it doesn't necessarily say discomfort.
- The chair had the glowing arrow, so kids would probably click it. The instructions help here, and saying "Tap the green chair" would make it clearer.

**Hold to keep eye open (green button)**
- The button looks pressable, but the hold action might not be understood.
- Only the voiceover says the button should be held.
- Kids might spam the button instead of holding it.
- **Suggestion:** turn the background black when the kid isn't holding the button, to simulate the eye being closed.

**Blink (purple button)**
- Pressing it to blink feels intuitive.

**End of game**
- The reward was a nice touch. It's easy to tell that a good action was taken, so success is clear.
- It's unclear which button to press after the game ends.

### General Feedback
- **There should always be a green button to move forward.** It should start glowing when the kid clicks the wrong thing, since young kids have short attention spans.
- Nothing felt uncomfortable.
- The animations are nice.
